How I research

The same sequence runs through my projects. Each step below has an example from work on this site.

Eight steps, each with an example

  1. Ask a question narrow enough to answer

    The juvenile justice topic became one population (sentenced prisoners aged 14 to under 18) and one right (family contact).

  2. State when the rule applies

    Before asking what courts did with the CISG, we tested whether it applied at all, step by step.

  3. Rank the sources

    A treaty, a committee's interpretation, a non-binding standard and a foreign statute carry different weight, and I say which is which.

  4. Compare on fixed criteria

    Seven questions asked of every source in the juvenile justice project, instead of a country-by-country summary.

  5. Check the evidence against the claim

    25 of 27 judgments not mentioning the CISG measured recognition, not interpretation, so we changed the claim.

  6. Write down competing readings

    Two readings of the misconduct rule, each with its own remedy.

  7. Make it usable

    A screening tool for the team; a staged list of reforms for decision-makers.

  8. Mark what is uncertain and revise

    The tags on this site do the same job: documented, self-reported, in development, not yet confirmed.

This describes how I worked on documented projects. I have not yet applied it to United States or EU technology law, which is where I want to use it next.
